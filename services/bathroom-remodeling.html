Complete bathroom renovations built for the demands of coastal living. Shower and tub conversions, custom tile, vanity replacement, and ADA accessibility modifications.

Full-Service Bathroom Renovation

What Our Bathroom Remodels Include

Every bathroom remodel we take on is tailored to how you actually use the space. Some homeowners want a dated tub ripped out and replaced with a modern walk-in shower. Others need a complete gut renovation down to the studs. We handle both, and everything in between.

Our bathroom remodeling projects typically include:

  • Tub-to-shower conversions - remove an existing bathtub and build a tiled walk-in shower with proper waterproofing, a recessed niche, and glass enclosure or curtain rod.
  • Shower tile installation - full tile surrounds using porcelain, ceramic, or natural stone. We handle layout, backer board, waterproof membrane, and grout.
  • Vanity and countertop replacement - single or double vanities with solid-surface, quartz, or granite countertops. We match your style and plumb everything to code.
  • Flooring - tile, luxury vinyl plank, or other moisture-resistant flooring installed over a properly prepared subfloor.
  • Plumbing fixture upgrades - new faucets, showerheads, valves, and supply lines. We coordinate plumbing rough-in and finish work.
  • Lighting - recessed can lights, vanity sconces, and shower-rated fixtures. Proper bathroom lighting changes the entire feel of the space.
  • Exhaust fan installation and upgrade - correctly sized and vented exhaust fans that actually move moisture out of the room. Many older coastal homes have undersized or improperly vented fans that create more problems than they solve.

We handle demolition, framing, plumbing, electrical coordination, tile, paint, and final finish work. One contractor, one point of contact, one responsibility chain from start to final walkthrough.

Built for the Oregon Coast

Moisture Control in Coastal Bathrooms

Bathrooms in coastal Oregon homes fail faster than anywhere else. The combination of high indoor humidity from showers and baths, elevated outdoor humidity from the Pacific, and salt-laden air creates conditions where moisture damage accelerates at two to three times the rate of inland homes. We see the consequences constantly - soft subfloors, mold behind tile, rotted framing around tub surrounds, and rusted-out hardware.

The root cause is almost always the same: the original bathroom was built with materials and methods that don't hold up in a marine environment. Standard drywall behind shower tile absorbs moisture that migrates through grout joints. Paper-faced drywall on bathroom ceilings grows mold within a few years. Inadequate exhaust fans leave moisture trapped in wall cavities where it feeds rot quietly for years before anyone notices.

When we remodel a coastal bathroom, we address all of this from the inside out:

  • Cement backer board behind all tile - never standard drywall. Cement board doesn't absorb water and won't degrade when moisture inevitably reaches it.
  • Sheet membrane waterproofing - a continuous waterproof barrier applied over the backer board before any tile goes up. This is the real moisture barrier, not the tile or grout.
  • Properly sized exhaust ventilation - we size the fan to the room's cubic footage, not just the square footage, and vent it directly to the exterior. No venting into the attic, no undersized units, no flex duct runs that sag and trap condensation.
  • Moisture-resistant materials throughout - PVC and composite trim instead of wood, marine-grade hardware, and mold-resistant drywall on ceilings and non-tiled walls.

These aren't upgrades - they're the baseline for how a bathroom should be built when you live within a few miles of the ocean. If your current bathroom wasn't built this way, a remodel is the opportunity to get it right.

Safe & Accessible Design

Aging-in-Place & Accessibility

A bathroom remodel is the best time to add accessibility features - whether you need them now or want to plan ahead. Retrofitting grab bars or widening a doorway later means tearing into walls and tile you just paid to install. Building them in during the remodel costs a fraction of what it would cost as a separate project.

We design and build ADA-compliant bathroom modifications that are functional, safe, and still look like a well-designed bathroom - not an institutional afterthought. Our aging-in-place features include:

  • Grab bars - structurally mounted into framing at the shower, tub, and toilet. Decorative finishes that match your fixtures so they blend into the design.
  • Barrier-free showers - zero-threshold or low-threshold entries that eliminate the step-over. Properly sloped floors with linear drains for wheelchair or walker access.
  • Comfort-height toilets - ADA-compliant 17- to 19-inch seat height that's easier to use for anyone with mobility limitations.
  • Wider doorways - reframed to 32 inches clear or wider for wheelchair and walker access. We handle the header, trim, and door replacement.
  • Non-slip flooring - textured tile or other slip-resistant surfaces rated for wet conditions, inside the shower and across the bathroom floor.
  • Accessible vanity heights - lowered or open-bottom vanities that accommodate seated use.

Many Lincoln County homeowners are planning to stay in their homes long-term. A bathroom that's safe to use at 75 is just as comfortable to use at 45 - it's simply better design. We'll walk you through the options during your estimate and help you decide which features make sense for your situation.
