Recognizing the Problem

When Your Deck Needs Repair

Decks on the Oregon Coast endure conditions that most inland structures never face. The combination of salt-laden air, 60+ inches of annual rainfall, persistent wind, and UV exposure during summer months creates an environment that accelerates deterioration in ways that are not always immediately visible. By the time you notice a problem on the surface, the damage underneath may have been progressing for years.

The most common signs that your deck needs professional attention include boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot, railings that wobble when you lean against them, fasteners that have popped up above the deck surface, and discoloration or dark staining that indicates moisture penetration. Warped or cupped boards, split ends, and splintering are also clear indicators that the wood has cycled through too many wet-dry seasons without adequate protection.

Coastal conditions accelerate every type of deck damage. Salt air corrodes metal fasteners from the inside out, often leaving screws and bolts structurally compromised even when they appear intact on the surface. The constant moisture cycling - where wood absorbs rain and then dries in coastal wind - causes expansion and contraction that loosens connections, opens gaps between boards, and creates entry points for rot. Moss and algae growth traps moisture against the wood surface, preventing it from drying fully between rain events and creating a perpetually damp environment where fungal decay thrives.

Many homeowners assume that a deck looking gray and weathered is purely cosmetic. In reality, that graying is the breakdown of surface wood fibers, and it often signals that the protective finish has failed entirely. Once the finish is gone, moisture migrates deeper into the wood with every rain, setting the stage for structural problems that are far more expensive to address than a timely repair would have been.

What We Do

Our Deck Repair Services

We handle every aspect of deck repair, from replacing a handful of damaged boards to rebuilding the structural framing underneath. Every project starts with a thorough assessment - we do not quote repairs without understanding the full scope of the problem, including what is happening beneath the deck surface where most of the critical damage hides.

Board replacement is the most common deck repair we perform. We remove damaged, rotted, or warped deck boards and replace them with matching material. For composite decks, we source boards from the same manufacturer and color line whenever possible. For wood decks, we use pressure-treated lumber rated for ground contact and coastal exposure. We check the joists beneath every removed board to confirm the substructure is still sound before installing new decking.

Structural joist and beam repair addresses the framing that supports your deck. When joists are cracked, rotted, or sagging, the entire deck surface above them is compromised. We sister new joists alongside damaged ones, replace beams that have lost their load-bearing capacity, and reinforce ledger board connections where the deck attaches to your house. Ledger board failure is one of the most dangerous deck conditions - it can cause a sudden collapse - and we inspect this connection on every repair project.

Railing repair covers loose, wobbly, or corroded railing systems. We replace rotted railing posts, install new balusters, and upgrade the hardware connecting posts to the deck frame. Railing integrity is a safety issue, not just a cosmetic one, and we ensure every repaired railing meets current Oregon building code requirements for height and load resistance.

Stair repair includes replacing cracked or rotted stair treads, rebuilding damaged stringers, and securing loose handrails. Deck stairs are often the first component to fail because they collect water on horizontal surfaces and their connection points are exposed to splash-back from the ground.

Refinishing and sealing restores your deck's appearance and protects the wood from further moisture damage. We power-wash the surface, remove moss and algae, sand rough areas, and apply a penetrating stain or sealant formulated for coastal conditions. Proper refinishing extends the life of a wood deck by years and prevents the moisture damage that leads to costly structural repairs later.

Post replacement addresses support posts that have rotted at ground level or lost their connection to the footings below. We install new posts with proper standoff hardware that keeps the wood separated from concrete, preventing the direct contact that causes posts to rot from the bottom up.

Coastal Construction Knowledge

Why Coastal Decks Fail Faster

Building and maintaining a deck on the Oregon Coast is fundamentally different from doing the same work 50 miles inland. The environmental stressors that coastal decks endure are relentless, and understanding them is essential to performing repairs that actually last rather than failing again within a few seasons.

Salt air corrosion is the hidden destroyer of coastal decks. The ocean releases salt particles into the air that travel miles inland, and these particles settle on every exposed surface. On metal fasteners - screws, bolts, joist hangers, and bracket hardware - salt accelerates oxidation dramatically. Standard galvanized fasteners that would last 20 years inland can fail in 5 to 8 years on the coast. We use stainless steel or hot-dipped galvanized hardware rated for marine environments on every coastal repair.

Moisture cycling is the mechanical process that physically destroys wood fiber. When wood absorbs rainwater, it swells. When coastal wind dries it out, it shrinks. This expansion and contraction happens hundreds of times per year on the Oregon Coast, and each cycle loosens fastener connections, opens cracks in board ends, and creates pathways for moisture to penetrate deeper into the wood structure. Over time, this cycling separates the wood grain and weakens the board from the inside out.

UV exposure during the summer months breaks down lignin - the natural compound that binds wood fibers together. When lignin degrades, the surface wood fibers loosen and wash away with rain, leaving the gray, roughened texture that characterizes neglected coastal decks. This is not just a cosmetic issue - it represents a progressive loss of material that thins the boards and reduces their structural capacity over time.

Moss and algae growth is both a slip hazard and a rot accelerator. The mild, damp climate of the Oregon Coast creates ideal conditions for biological growth on horizontal wood surfaces. Moss holds moisture against the wood like a sponge, preventing the surface from ever fully drying. Underneath that moss layer, the wood stays permanently damp, which is exactly the condition that wood-decay fungi need to establish and spread. Cleaning moss off a deck is not just about appearance - it is a structural maintenance task.

Trapped moisture between boards is a design issue that many older decks share. When boards are installed too tightly, or when debris accumulates in the gaps between them, water cannot drain away from the joist tops. The joists sit in persistent dampness, and because they are hidden from view and airflow, they rot silently until a board gives way underfoot. Proper repair includes clearing debris channels and ensuring adequate drainage gaps between all deck boards.

Common Questions

Deck Repair FAQ

In most cases, we can replace individual boards without tearing out the entire deck. We inspect the substructure beneath the damaged boards to confirm the joists and framing are still solid. If the damage is limited to the surface, a partial board replacement is a cost-effective repair. If we find rot or structural compromise underneath, we will explain the full scope before proceeding.

Warning signs include a bouncy or spongy feel when walking, visible sagging between posts, cracked or split beams, posts that wobble or lean, and soft spots around fastener locations. If you can push a screwdriver into a joist or beam with little resistance, the wood has likely rotted internally. We offer structural inspections to assess framing, ledger board connections, and post footings before damage spreads.

The most common cause on the Oregon Coast is corrosion of the fasteners and hardware that connect the railing posts to the deck frame. Salt-laden air accelerates rust on screws, bolts, and metal brackets. Additionally, wood shrinkage from repeated wet-dry cycles loosens connections over time. We repair loose railings by replacing corroded hardware with stainless steel or hot-dipped galvanized fasteners and reinforcing the post-to-frame connection.

Wood decks on the Oregon Coast should be cleaned and re-sealed every one to two years. The combination of heavy rainfall, salt air, UV exposure, and moss growth breaks down protective finishes faster than inland locations. We recommend a penetrating oil-based stain rather than a film-forming finish, as it handles moisture cycling better and does not peel. We can refinish your deck as part of a repair project or as a standalone maintenance service.
