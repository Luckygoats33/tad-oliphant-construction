Building a New Home on the Oregon Coast: What to Expect

8 min read

Living on the Oregon Coast is a privilege that comes with unique responsibilities. The same dramatic beauty that draws people here, the crashing waves, the misty forests, the wild Pacific storms, also creates an environment that is constantly working against your home. Understanding these forces and how to work with them is essential for any coastal homeowner.

At Tad Oliphant Construction, we have spent more than two decades building and maintaining homes in this environment. Everything we do is informed by our experience with the specific challenges that Lincoln County properties face.

The Coastal Environment

Oregon's central coast receives between 60 and 80 inches of rain per year, with most of it falling between October and April. Winter storms regularly bring sustained winds of 40 to 60 mph with gusts exceeding 80 mph. Salt air penetrates several miles inland from the ocean, corroding metal and degrading unprotected surfaces.

Humidity levels remain high year-round, often exceeding 80 percent during winter months. This persistent moisture creates ideal conditions for mold, mildew, and wood-destroying fungi. Homes that are not built or maintained with these conditions in mind will deteriorate much faster than their inland counterparts.

Building for the Coast

Successful coastal construction comes down to three principles: keep water out, let moisture escape, and use materials that can handle what gets through. Every detail matters. The type of flashing around a window, the grade of fastener in a deck, the ventilation design in a crawl space. These decisions determine whether a home thrives or struggles in our environment.

We build and renovate with these principles at the forefront of every project. When we recommend a specific material or technique, it is because we have seen it perform in our specific conditions. Our recommendations are based on real-world results, not manufacturer marketing.

Seasonal Considerations

Spring and summer are the ideal seasons for exterior construction on the coast. The drier weather allows for proper application of paints, stains, and sealants, and the longer days give us more productive work hours. However, we work year-round and can tackle many projects during the fall and winter months, particularly interior work and emergency repairs.

If you are planning a project, we recommend starting the conversation in winter or early spring so we can schedule your work during the optimal weather window. Our schedule fills up quickly during the summer months.

Protecting Your Investment

Regular maintenance is the most cost-effective way to protect your coastal home. Annual inspections of your roof, siding, windows, decks, and foundation can catch small problems before they become expensive repairs. We offer maintenance consultations for homeowners who want professional guidance on what to watch for and when to take action.
