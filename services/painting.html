Our Painting Services

Whether you're refreshing the interior of a beach cottage or protecting the exterior of a two-story home against coastal weather, we approach every painting project the same way - with thorough preparation and the right materials for the environment. Painting on the Oregon Coast isn't the same as painting in Portland or the Willamette Valley. The constant moisture, salt-laden air, and UV exposure mean that shortcuts show up fast. We take the time to do it right because we've seen what happens when contractors don't.

  • Exterior house painting - full prep, prime, and multi-coat application for siding, trim, eaves, and fascia boards
  • Interior rooms and ceilings - walls, ceilings, closets, and accent features with clean lines and consistent coverage
  • Trim and detail work - window frames, door casings, baseboards, crown molding, and built-in cabinetry
  • Cabinet painting - proper deglossing, bonding primer, and spray or brush finish for kitchen and bathroom cabinets
  • Deck and fence staining - penetrating stains and sealers for pressure-treated lumber, cedar, and composite surfaces
  • Power washing before painting - remove mildew, algae, loose paint, and salt deposits before any surface preparation begins
  • Caulking and sealing - exterior-grade sealants at all joints, window perimeters, and transitions to prevent water intrusion

We handle projects of all sizes - from a single room refresh to a complete exterior repaint on a multi-story home. Every project starts with an honest assessment of what your surfaces need before any paint goes on.

Why Prep Work Matters on the Coast

Paint failure on the Oregon Coast almost always starts with poor preparation - not bad paint. The cycle works like this: moisture wicks into bare or poorly sealed wood through cracks, nail holes, and open joints. Salt air accelerates corrosion on metal surfaces and breaks down the bond between paint film and substrate. UV radiation degrades the resin binders in paint, causing chalking and fading. When you paint over a surface that hasn't been properly cleaned, scraped, and primed, you're trapping moisture underneath the new coat. Within a year or two, that moisture pushes outward and the paint blisters, peels, and cracks - starting the cycle over again.

Our preparation process addresses every point of failure before we open a can of paint. We start with a pressure wash to remove surface mildew, algae, salt deposits, and chalked paint residue. Then we hand-scrape and sand every surface back to a sound substrate - if the old paint is flaking, blistering, or poorly adhered, it all comes off. We fill cracks, dents, and nail holes with exterior-grade wood filler. We replace any caulking that has pulled away from joints, window frames, or trim transitions. And we apply a quality bonding primer to all bare wood, repaired areas, and surfaces that have been sanded to raw substrate.

For homes built before 1978, lead paint is a real concern. Many older coastal homes - especially in Newport, Lincoln City, and Toledo - still have layers of lead-based paint on siding, trim, and window sashes. Tad Oliphant Construction is EPA-certified for lead paint renovation, repair, and painting under the RRP Rule. We follow proper containment procedures, use HEPA-filtered equipment, and handle cleanup and disposal according to federal guidelines. If you're planning to repaint an older home and aren't sure about lead, we can test suspect surfaces before we start work. This isn't a formality - it's required by law, and it protects your family.

We also won't paint in bad conditions. If the forecast calls for rain within 24 hours, if surfaces are damp from morning dew or fog, or if temperatures are below 50 degrees Fahrenheit, we wait. Applying paint to a wet surface or in high humidity causes adhesion failure and traps moisture underneath the film. That means some days we might show up, check conditions, and reschedule. That's not wasted time - it's the discipline that separates a five-year paint job from a two-year one.

Coastal-Grade Paint and Application

Not all paint performs the same in a marine environment. The products we use for exterior work on the Oregon Coast are specifically formulated to resist the conditions that destroy cheaper coatings. For exterior siding and trim, we use 100% acrylic latex paints with built-in UV stabilizers, mildew inhibitors, and flexible film properties that expand and contract with temperature swings without cracking. Acrylic latex outperforms oil-based paints in nearly every coastal application - it breathes better, allowing trapped moisture to escape rather than blister, and it maintains color and sheen longer under UV exposure.

There are exceptions. Oil-based and alkyd primers still perform better as a first coat over bare wood, rusted metal, and stain-blocking applications. We use them where they make sense - particularly on knotty cedar trim, bare pressure-treated lumber that has dried for at least six months, and metal flashing or railings where adhesion and corrosion resistance matter most. The combination of an oil-based primer under acrylic latex topcoats gives you the adhesion and stain-blocking of oil with the durability and flexibility of latex.

Application thickness matters as much as product selection. We apply exterior topcoats at the manufacturer's recommended mil thickness - typically 4 to 5 mils wet, drying to 1.5 to 2 mils per coat. Two full topcoats over primer is our standard for exterior work, and on south- and west-facing walls with maximum sun and storm exposure, we may recommend a third coat for additional protection. We apply by brush, roller, or airless spray depending on the surface - brushwork for trim and detail areas, spray for large siding expanses followed by back-brushing to work the paint into the substrate.

We typically recommend Sherwin-Williams Duration or SuperPaint for exterior applications and Benjamin Moore Regal Select for interior work. These are professional-grade products with proven track records in coastal climates. The upfront cost difference between premium and economy paint is small compared to the labor cost of repainting two or three years early. With proper prep and quality materials, you should expect 5 to 7 years of exterior life on the coast - longer for protected surfaces, shorter for fully exposed south-facing walls that take the worst of the weather.
