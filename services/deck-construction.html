Our Work

What Our Deck Construction Includes

Every deck we build in Lincoln County starts with the structure and ends with the details. We handle the full scope of work from footings and framing through decking, railings, stairs, and final inspection. Whether you want a simple platform deck off the back door or a multi-level outdoor living space with built-in seating and lighting, we build it to code and built to last in coastal conditions.

Composite Decking

As a Fiberon certified installer, we build with Fiberon Symmetry, Good Life, and Concordia lines. Composite boards resist moisture, fading, and salt air damage without annual staining or sealing. We match the board profile and color to your home's exterior for a finished look that holds up for 25+ years on the coast.

Wood Decking

We work with Western red cedar, redwood, and pressure-treated Douglas fir for homeowners who prefer the look and feel of natural wood. Every wood deck we build gets proper end-grain sealer on all cuts and a penetrating stain to protect against Lincoln County's 60+ inches of annual rainfall. We will walk you through the maintenance schedule so there are no surprises.

Railings & Stairs

We install aluminum, composite, cable, and glass railing systems. Stair construction includes proper stringer calculation, rise-and-run compliance with Oregon building code, and anti-slip treads for wet conditions. Every railing meets the 36-inch residential height requirement and the 4-inch baluster spacing code.

Outdoor Living Spaces

Decks are just the starting point. We build integrated outdoor kitchens, pergola-covered seating areas, hot tub pads with reinforced framing, and fire pit surrounds. If the project involves electrical or plumbing, we coordinate with licensed subcontractors and pull the appropriate permits through Lincoln County.

Permits & Engineering

Lincoln County requires a building permit for all new deck construction. Decks over 30 inches above grade need engineered plans and guardrails. We prepare the plan set, submit to the county building department, and schedule all required inspections. You never have to visit the permit office or manage the paperwork yourself.

Footings & Foundations

Coastal soil conditions vary widely across Lincoln County. We pour concrete footings sized to your soil's bearing capacity and set Simpson Strong-Tie post bases to keep framing lumber off the ground. In areas with soft or sandy soil, we use deeper footings or helical piers to prevent settling over time.

Coastal Engineering

Built for the Oregon Coast

Building a deck in Lincoln County is not the same as building one in the Willamette Valley. The Oregon Coast puts more stress on outdoor structures than almost anywhere else in the Pacific Northwest. Salt-laden air corrodes standard fasteners within a few years. Over 60 inches of rain per year saturates wood framing and accelerates rot. Sustained winter winds of 40 to 60 mph put lateral loads on railings and overhead structures that inland decks never face.

Every material choice we make accounts for these conditions. We use stainless steel or triple-coated structural screws rated for coastal exposure. Ledger board connections get self-adhering flashing tape and proper spacing to prevent moisture trapping against the house sheathing. Joist tape goes on every horizontal framing member to stop water from pooling on the top edge of joists, which is the number-one cause of premature deck framing failure on the coast.

Fiberon composite decking is engineered for exactly this environment. The boards have a protective PermaTech cap layer that resists moisture absorption, UV fading, and mold growth. Unlike natural wood, composite will not cup, split, or check after repeated wet-dry cycles. The color holds for the life of the product without sanding or restaining. For homeowners who want a deck that looks the same in year ten as it did the day it was built, composite is the clear choice for coastal Oregon.

We also design for drainage and airflow. Proper joist spacing, adequate ground clearance, and strategic placement of ventilation gaps beneath the deck surface keep air moving and moisture from accumulating. In areas prone to standing water, we install under-deck drainage systems that channel runoff away from the foundation and protect the space below the deck for storage or additional usable area.

How We Work

Our Deck Building Process

We follow the same five-step process on every deck project. It keeps the job organized, on schedule, and free of surprises for the homeowner.

1. On-Site Consultation

Tad meets you at your property to assess the site, discuss your goals, take measurements, and review any existing structural conditions. We look at soil type, slope, sun exposure, access to the house, and any trees or utilities that may affect the layout. This visit is free and comes with no obligation.

2. Design & Estimate

We prepare a detailed written estimate that breaks down materials, labor, and timeline. For complex projects, we provide a scaled site plan showing the proposed deck layout, stair placement, and railing configuration. You choose the decking material, color, and railing style before we move forward.

3. Permitting

We submit the required plans to Lincoln County Building Department and obtain the building permit. For elevated decks, we work with a licensed engineer to produce stamped structural drawings. Most permits are approved within 5 to 10 business days.

4. Construction

Our crew handles every phase of the build: demolition of the old deck (if applicable), footing excavation and concrete pours, framing, decking installation, railing and stair construction, and trim work. We keep the job site clean and communicate progress daily. A standard deck takes 2 to 4 weeks depending on size and complexity.

5. Final Walkthrough & Inspection

We schedule the final building inspection with Lincoln County, walk the completed deck with you to confirm everything meets your expectations, and provide care instructions for your specific decking material. Composite decks require minimal upkeep; wood decks get a written maintenance schedule so you know exactly what to do and when.

More Services

Related Services

Deck Repair

Rotting boards, loose railings, sinking footings, and structural damage. We repair and restore existing decks to safe, solid condition. If your deck needs more than a few boards replaced, we will give you an honest assessment of whether repair or full rebuild makes more sense.

Dry-Rot Repair

Coastal moisture breeds dry rot in framing, siding, and trim. We remove all compromised wood, treat surrounding framing with borate preservative, and rebuild with materials rated for the conditions. Deck ledger boards and rim joists are common rot locations we inspect and repair regularly.

Fencing

Privacy fencing, wind barriers, and property-line enclosures built with the same coastal-grade materials and fasteners we use on decks. Cedar, composite, and vinyl fence options designed to handle Lincoln County wind and weather year-round.

Painting

Exterior and interior painting for homes across Lincoln County. For wood decks, we apply penetrating stains that protect against UV and moisture without peeling or flaking in wet conditions. We also paint and stain pergolas, railings, and other outdoor structures.
