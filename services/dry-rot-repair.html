Our Process

How We Find and Fix Dry Rot

Dry rot rarely stays where you can see it. The soft spot under a window sill or the discolored trim around a door frame is almost always the surface expression of damage that runs deeper into the wall cavity, framing, or subfloor. Fixing only what's visible is how homeowners end up paying for the same repair twice.

Our process starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and physical probing to map the full extent of compromised wood before any demolition begins. We check behind siding, inside wall cavities, along rim joists, and around every penetration point where water may have entered. This tells us exactly how far the damage goes - not just where it started.

Once we know the full scope, we remove all affected material back to sound, dry wood. There's no guessing and no cutting corners here. If a structural member is compromised - a rim joist, a stud, a header - we sister new framing alongside it or replace it entirely, depending on the severity. Every piece of replacement lumber is cut, fitted, and fastened to meet or exceed the original structural specifications.

After the new material is in place, we install moisture barriers, seal all joints and transitions, and verify that the assembly is properly ventilated to prevent trapped moisture from restarting the cycle. The finished repair is structurally sound, weather-tight, and built to handle another decade of coastal conditions.

Coastal Reality

Why Dry Rot Is Everywhere on the Oregon Coast

Lincoln County averages over 60 inches of rain per year. Add salt-laden Pacific air, persistent fog cycles that keep surfaces damp for days, and condensation that forms inside wall cavities when interior heat meets cold exterior sheathing - and you have an environment that actively promotes wood decay in any structure that isn't perfectly sealed and ventilated.

Many homes along the central Oregon Coast were built decades ago with ventilation details and moisture management practices that don't hold up to modern understanding. Original builder's felt breaks down. Flashing was omitted at critical transitions. Caulk joints failed years ago and were never re-sealed. The result is water finding its way behind siding, around window frames, and into structural framing where it sits, unseen, feeding fungal growth for months or years before any exterior sign appears.

The most common failure points we encounter are window sills and jambs, exterior door frames and thresholds, rim joists where floor framing meets the foundation wall, deck ledger boards bolted to the house, bottom plates behind siding at grade level, and fascia boards where gutters have overflowed or leaked. If your home is more than 15 years old and sits within a few miles of the coast, there's a good chance at least one of these areas has some degree of moisture damage - even if everything looks fine from the outside.

We've been working exclusively in Lincoln County for over a decade, and we've seen these patterns repeat on hundreds of homes in Siletz, Newport, Lincoln City, Toledo, Depoe Bay, Waldport, and South Beach. That experience means we know where to look first, what to expect behind the surface, and how to build repairs that last in this specific climate.

Long-Term Protection

Preventing Future Rot Damage

Repairing rot without addressing the conditions that caused it guarantees the problem will return. Every dry-rot repair we complete includes a prevention strategy tailored to your home's specific exposure, construction type, and the original failure that allowed moisture in.

Moisture barriers and proper flashing are the first line of defense. We install housewrap, peel-and-stick membranes, and metal flashing at every horizontal joint, window and door opening, deck ledger connection, and roof-to-wall transition. These details are what keep bulk water from reaching the wood structure behind your siding and trim - and they're the details that were most often skipped or done poorly in older coastal construction.

Ventilation is equally critical. Wall cavities need airflow to dry out the moisture that inevitably gets past the exterior skin. We verify that rainscreen gaps, soffit vents, and crawl space ventilation are functioning correctly. When they aren't - which is common - we correct them as part of the repair to ensure trapped moisture has a path to escape rather than accumulating against the framing.

Material selection matters on the coast. We use borate-treated lumber for structural replacements in high-exposure areas, which resists fungal growth at the cellular level. Exterior trim is replaced with composite, PVC, or primed finger-joint material depending on the application. All end grain and field cuts are sealed before installation to prevent moisture wicking into exposed wood fibers. We also recommend maintenance intervals - recaulking, repainting, gutter cleaning - specific to your home's location and how directly it faces the prevailing weather.

Common Questions

Dry-Rot Repair FAQ

Dry rot is caused by a specific fungus (Serpula lacrymans) that can spread through wood even in relatively low-moisture conditions once established. Wet rot requires consistently high moisture to progress and stays localized to the damp area. Dry rot is more dangerous because it can travel through masonry and framing to reach dry wood elsewhere in the structure. Both types require professional removal and repair, but dry rot demands more aggressive investigation to ensure the fungus hasn't spread beyond the visible damage.

Warning signs include paint that bubbles or peels in localized areas, wood that feels soft or spongy when pressed, a persistent musty smell near walls or windows, doors or windows that suddenly stick or won't close properly, and visible discoloration or darkening of wood surfaces. On the Oregon Coast, the most common locations for hidden rot are window sills, door thresholds, rim joists, deck ledger boards, and anywhere siding meets trim. If you notice any of these signs, a professional inspection with moisture meters and probing tools can determine the full extent of damage behind the surface.

In most cases, standard homeowner's insurance does not cover dry-rot repair because insurers classify it as a maintenance issue rather than sudden damage. However, if rot resulted from a covered event - such as a storm that damaged your roof and allowed water intrusion - the resulting rot damage may be partially covered. We recommend contacting your insurance provider before starting repairs. We can provide detailed documentation of the damage, including photos and a written scope of work, which may help support a claim if the rot is connected to a covered incident.

Prevention starts during the repair itself. We install proper moisture barriers and flashing at every joint and transition point, ensure adequate ventilation in wall cavities and crawl spaces, and select materials rated for high-moisture environments. We use borate-treated lumber where appropriate, which is naturally resistant to fungal growth. After the repair, we seal all end grain and exposed cuts, verify that drainage slopes away from the structure, and check that gutters and downspouts are directing water clear of the foundation. We also provide maintenance recommendations specific to your home's exposure and construction.
