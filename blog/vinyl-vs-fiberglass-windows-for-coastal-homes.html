Vinyl vs Fiberglass Windows for Coastal Homes

6 min read

Windows and doors are critical components of any coastal home. They provide natural light, ventilation, views, and access while also serving as barriers against wind, rain, and salt air. On the Oregon Coast, choosing the right windows and doors and ensuring proper installation is essential for both comfort and long-term durability.

Tad Oliphant Construction provides window and door replacement services throughout Lincoln County. We work with leading manufacturers and install products rated for coastal conditions, ensuring your investment performs well for decades.

Choosing Windows for Coastal Homes

For Oregon Coast homes, we recommend vinyl or fiberglass frame windows with dual-pane low-E glass. These materials resist moisture, salt corrosion, and UV degradation while providing excellent thermal performance. Wood-clad windows can also work well if the exterior cladding is aluminum or fiberglass.

Avoid all-wood frame windows in direct coastal exposure unless you are committed to rigorous maintenance. We have seen many beautiful wood windows deteriorate rapidly once the paint or finish begins to fail in our harsh environment.

Energy Efficiency

Modern windows make a dramatic difference in energy efficiency. If your home has original single-pane windows, upgrading to dual-pane low-E windows can reduce heat loss through the glass by 50 percent or more. This translates to real savings on heating costs, which is significant during our long, cool coastal winters.

Many of our clients also qualify for utility rebates and tax credits when upgrading to energy-efficient windows. We can help you understand the available incentives during our initial consultation.

Installation Quality

Even the best windows will fail if they are not installed correctly. Proper installation includes waterproof flashing around the entire window opening, backer rod and sealant at all joints, proper shimming for plumb and level operation, and insulation between the window frame and the rough opening. We follow manufacturer installation guidelines precisely and stand behind our work.

Storm Doors and Entry Doors

Entry doors on the coast need to resist warping, swelling, and corrosion. We recommend fiberglass entry doors for most coastal applications. They resist moisture, will not warp, and can be finished to look like real wood. Steel doors are also an option but require careful attention to weather stripping and finish maintenance to prevent rust.

Sliding glass doors and French doors should feature marine-grade hardware and multi-point locking systems for both security and weather resistance. We install products from manufacturers who rate their hardware for coastal conditions.
