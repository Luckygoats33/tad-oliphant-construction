Comprehensive Siding Solutions

What Our Siding Services Include

Whether you need one damaged section replaced or an entire home re-sided, we handle every step from tear-off through final paint.

Our siding work covers the full range of what coastal homeowners encounter. That includes complete siding removal and replacement when the existing material has failed, partial repairs where storm damage or localized rot has compromised a section of wall, and emergency board-up and restoration after major weather events.

We install LP SmartSide, HardiePlank (James Hardie fiber cement), and engineered wood siding. Each material has trade-offs in cost, durability, appearance, and maintenance - and those trade-offs shift significantly when you're building on the Oregon Coast versus inland. We walk you through the options for your specific house and exposure before you commit to anything.

Every siding project starts with a thorough inspection of the substrate. We probe for soft spots, check moisture levels in the sheathing, and examine the condition of the house wrap or moisture barrier underneath. If we find rot - and on older coastal homes, we often do - we address the structural damage before new siding goes on. Covering up compromised sheathing or framing with fresh siding is a mistake we won't make, because it guarantees the problem returns worse in a few years.

We also evaluate cedar siding for homeowners who prefer a traditional look. Cedar performs well when maintained, but the coastal environment demands more frequent staining and sealing than most homeowners expect. If you have existing cedar siding that's showing signs of cupping, splitting, or graying, we'll assess whether it can be restored or whether switching to an engineered product will save you money over time.

Hidden Damage Detection

What We Find Behind the Siding

The most expensive problems on a coastal home are the ones you can't see from the outside.

Lincoln County averages over 60 inches of rain per year, and many homes sit in microclimates where fog, salt air, and wind-driven rain create constant moisture pressure on exterior walls. That moisture finds every gap - around window and door flashing, at horizontal joints where siding overlaps, through aging caulk lines, and along the bottom edge where siding meets the foundation.

When we remove old siding, we regularly find failed moisture barriers where the house wrap has torn, bunched, or degraded over time. Water that gets past the siding and past a failed barrier soaks directly into the plywood or OSB sheathing. Left unchecked, that sheathing rots, and the damage spreads into the wall framing - studs, headers, and rim joists.

Insect damage compounds the problem. Carpenter ants and moisture-loving beetles follow the damp wood, hollowing out framing members that look solid from the outside. We've pulled off siding on homes where the exterior appeared fine but the wall studs behind it were barely holding the structure together.

This is why we never quote a siding job purely from the outside. We probe suspect areas, measure moisture content in accessible sheathing, and build our estimate with contingency for what we expect to find underneath. When we do find damage, we photograph it, show you exactly what we're dealing with, and explain the repair before proceeding. No one likes discovering hidden rot, but finding it during a planned siding project is far better than learning about it when a wall starts to sag.

Material Guide

Choosing the Right Siding for the Coast

Not every siding material holds up the same way in salt air, 60+ inches of rain, and sustained Pacific winds.

LP SmartSide is our most-recommended product for Oregon Coast homes. It's an engineered wood siding treated with SmartGuard - a zinc borate process that protects against rot, termites, and fungal decay at the core level. It holds paint exceptionally well, resists impact damage better than fiber cement, and installs faster because it's lighter and easier to cut. LP SmartSide carries a 50-year substrate warranty, and in our experience it handles the coastal moisture cycle without the cracking or chipping issues that fiber cement can develop over time.

HardiePlank (James Hardie fiber cement) is the other top-tier option. Made from cement, sand, and cellulose fiber, it won't rot, warp, or burn. It's extremely durable against physical impact and offers a convincing wood-grain texture. The trade-offs are weight - fiber cement is heavy, which means higher labor costs - and the fact that it can crack if impacted at the wrong angle during installation or from settlement. HardiePlank comes with a 30-year non-prorated warranty and performs reliably in coastal environments when installed correctly with proper gapping and flashing.

Cedar is the traditional choice, and it still works well on the coast if you commit to the maintenance schedule. That means staining or sealing every 3-5 years - closer to every 2-3 years on ocean-facing walls with direct salt spray exposure. Cedar breathes well and handles moisture cycles naturally, but neglected cedar deteriorates fast in our climate. We install new cedar siding for homeowners who want the authentic look and understand the maintenance commitment.
