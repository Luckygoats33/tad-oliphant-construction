Framing & Structural Repair in Lincoln County, OR

Foundation-adjacent framing, load-bearing wall modifications, post and beam replacement, and earthquake preparedness for Oregon Coast homes.

Our Structural Repair Services

Structural framing is the skeleton of your home. When it fails, everything above it shifts, cracks, and deteriorates. We handle the full range of structural framing work for residential properties throughout Lincoln County, from targeted joist repairs to complete reframing of compromised wall and floor systems.

Sistered joists and rafters - When a floor joist or roof rafter has been weakened by rot, insect damage, or excessive loading, we bolt or nail a new full-length member alongside the damaged one. This restores the original load capacity without tearing out the existing structure, which keeps the project faster and less invasive.

Post and beam replacement - Support posts in crawl spaces and basements bear the weight of your entire floor system. When they rot at the base, split, or sit on inadequate footings, we replace them with new treated posts set on poured concrete pads. Beams that have cracked, sagged, or rotted get swapped for properly sized replacements - often engineered lumber that resists moisture better than the original dimensional lumber.

Load-bearing wall modifications - Whether you want to open up a kitchen, widen a hallway, or create a great room, we engineer and install the header or beam needed to carry the load once the wall is removed. Every load-bearing modification includes temporary shoring, proper engineering calculations, and a building permit.

Rim joist repair - The rim joist sits at the perimeter of your floor system and is one of the first framing members to rot on coastal homes because of its exposure to exterior moisture. We remove damaged sections, treat adjacent framing, and install new rim joist material with proper flashing and sealing.

Crawl space framing - Moisture, standing water, and poor ventilation make crawl spaces the most common location for structural rot in Lincoln County homes. We repair or replace floor joists, support posts, girders, and sill plates - and address the moisture conditions that caused the failure in the first place.

Subfloor replacement - When plywood or OSB subflooring has delaminated, softened, or developed mold from chronic moisture exposure, we remove the damaged panels, inspect and repair the joists beneath, and install new subflooring rated for the conditions.

Header replacement - Headers above windows and doors carry the weight of the wall and roof above them. When they crack, sag, or rot, you will see the wall shift above the opening. We shore the load, remove the failed header, and install a correctly sized replacement - often upgrading from the undersized headers found in older construction.

Why Coastal Homes Need Structural Attention

Lincoln County sits on the edge of the Pacific, and the conditions that make this coastline beautiful are the same ones that quietly dismantle the framing inside your walls. If your home is more than twenty years old, the odds of hidden structural damage are higher than most homeowners expect.

Moisture-driven rot in critical framing - Over sixty inches of annual rainfall combined with salt-laden fog creates a relentless moisture environment. Water finds its way behind siding, under flashing, and into crawl spaces where it saturates the framing members your home depends on. The rot often starts at connection points - where joists meet rim joists, where posts sit on concrete, where headers contact rough openings - and by the time it is visible from the outside, the structural damage is already significant.

Earthquake risk in the Cascadia subduction zone - The Cascadia subduction zone runs directly offshore, and geologists project a major earthquake within the next fifty years. Many homes along the Lincoln County coast were built before modern seismic codes were adopted, which means they lack cripple wall bracing, foundation bolting, and the shear connections that help a structure survive lateral forces. Retrofitting these elements now is far less expensive than rebuilding after a seismic event.

Older construction methods that don't meet current code - Homes built in the 1960s through the 1980s often have undersized headers, inadequate post-to-beam connections, and framing practices that were standard at the time but fall short of today's structural requirements. When these homes develop problems, the fix is not just replacing the damaged wood - it is bringing the connections and member sizes up to current standards so the repair actually lasts.

Settling and shifting from coastal soil conditions - The sandy and clay-heavy soils along the central Oregon Coast are prone to settling, especially when saturated by heavy rains or altered by drainage changes. When the ground beneath a home shifts, the framing above it absorbs the stress - posts tilt, beams deflect, and floors slope. Addressing the framing alone is not enough; we identify whether the soil movement is ongoing and whether footings need to be improved as part of the structural repair.

Our Approach to Structural Work

Structural repairs are not cosmetic work. What you see on the surface - a cracked wall, a sloping floor, a sticking door - is only the symptom. The actual failure is inside the wall cavity, under the subfloor, or down in the crawl space. Our process is designed to find the full extent of the problem before we start cutting and to leave your home structurally sound when we are done.

Thorough assessment before quoting - We do not guess at structural problems. Before we write an estimate, we inspect the area of concern, probe framing members for rot, measure deflection, and assess load paths. If we cannot determine the full scope from the exterior, we will recommend targeted exploratory openings so you are not surprised by what we find once work begins.

Temporary shoring and load management - Before removing any structural member, we install temporary shoring to carry the load safely during the repair. This is non-negotiable on every structural job we do. Rushing past this step is how walls crack, floors drop, and repairs create new damage. We shore conservatively and leave the supports in place until the permanent repair is fully installed and secured.

Code-compliant repairs - Every structural repair we perform meets or exceeds the current Oregon Residential Specialty Code. We use appropriately rated hardware, properly sized lumber, and approved fastening methods. When we sister a joist, we follow the nailing schedule. When we install a beam, it sits on properly sized bearing points. Code compliance is not an upgrade - it is the baseline.

Working with engineers when needed - Some structural modifications - particularly load-bearing wall removals, beam sizing for open spans, and seismic retrofit designs - require a stamped engineering plan. We work with licensed structural engineers regularly and can coordinate the engineering on your behalf. If your project needs an engineer's stamp for permitting, we will tell you upfront rather than guessing at the design.

Permitting - Structural work in Lincoln County requires a building permit and inspections. We handle the entire permitting process, from submitting the application and plans to scheduling the required inspections. You do not need to visit the county building department yourself.

Exposing and addressing the full extent of damage - Rot and structural failure rarely stay contained. A single soft rim joist usually means adjacent joists are also compromised. A failed post often means the beam it supported has deflected, affecting the subfloor and joists above it. We follow the damage until we find solid wood and sound structure, then work back from there. This means the final scope sometimes grows beyond the initial estimate - but we communicate every finding before we proceed, so you always know what you are paying for and why.
